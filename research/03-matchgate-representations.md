# Matchgate representations of the carry network

**Working arguments.** This note defines the representation being studied and records its limits. The [technical drafts](technical-drafts.md) contain the expanded proofs and basis-change calculations. The executable checker verifies the explicit open-chain transformation and the rank inputs for the transformed triangle. Independent proof review is pending.

## What is being represented

For target bit $t$, define

$$
T_t(a,x,y,d)=\mathbf 1[x+y+a=t+2d],
$$

where $a,d$ are the incoming and outgoing carries. Contract the carry wires and leave all selection ports open, in the natural bit order. A full instance also identifies the two selection ports belonging to each weight. Write the open arithmetic signature as $U$ and the equality signature as $E$. The number of subsets is their bilinear pairing. A change of basis $A$ on the open ports gives the pairing of $AU$ with $A^{-\mathsf{T}}E$.

Here a matchgate is a planar matchgate signature in a fixed order of its ports, equivalently a parity-definite fermionic Gaussian signature. The bounds below concern the number of terms in an explicit sum of such signatures with a common port order. For the background see [Cai and Gorenstein, *Matchgates Revisited*](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v010a007/), and [Bravyi, *Contraction of matchgate tensor networks on non-planar graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2989).

## Separate changes on individual ports

In the original basis, some repeated carry blocks restrict to copies of the four-mode vector $|0000\rangle+|1111\rangle$. Majorana-generated ranks then give an exponential lower bound on the number of matchgate terms, even with signed or complex coefficients. This uses the Gaussian-rank method developed in [Wei et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02172). A Hadamard on each of the four modes turns the isolated vector into the uniform even signature. The isolated block is therefore insufficient for a basis-independent obstruction.

The stronger test uses three active columns with target bits $(0,0,1)$ in least-significant-first order and two exposed ports in each column. Four separator columns can fix both exterior carries to zero, using parity-definite caps, for arbitrary invertible single-port changes of basis. Across the cut after the third exposed port, the active block has matrix rank three. The partial weights are $(1,1,2)$ on one side and $(2,4,4)$ on the other. To reach four, the two partial sums must be $(0,4),(2,2),(4,0)$, giving three outer products with disjoint row and column supports. A separate invertible change on each port preserves this rank.

The detailed working argument uses the first Majorana span, generated by a signature and its images under single Majorana operators. A six-mode vector of cut rank three has first span of dimension at least ten. Briefly, if the dimension is less than ten, parity embedding and elimination of isotropic annihilators make the embedded vector Gaussian. In the original six modes this gives a displaced Gaussian of the form $(1+\ell)e^Q$ after a suitable basis chart. Its ordinary rank across a consecutive cut is a power of two. The [technical derivation](technical-drafts.md#uniform-bound-for-all-local-bases) proves the annihilator and cut-rank steps.

On $r$ contiguous active blocks, the independent first-degree images multiply, with Jordan–Wigner signs accounted for by parity on the preceding blocks. A Gaussian signature on $6r$ modes has cumulative Clifford span at degree $d$ bounded by $\sum_{j\le d}\binom{6r}j$. Weighting the cumulative degree bounds by powers of $z$ gives

$$
R\ \ge\ \left(\frac{1+9z}{(1+z)^6}\right)^r.
$$

Choosing $z=1/15$ yields a base greater than $1.086$ for arbitrary invertible changes on individual ports. The detailed drafts also give stronger certificates for uniform Hadamards and generic bases. These bounds refer to the repeated open signatures with the specified terminal order.

## Joint changes within a column

Joint invertible changes on pairs of selection ports give a different outcome. In the order $(00,10,01,11)$, let

$$
A_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&2\\2&1&1&0\\0&-1&1&2\\-2&1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A_1=A_0P_{00,11},
$$

where $P_{00,11}$ exchanges the first and last coordinates. In the exterior-algebra variables ordered as $a,x,y,d$, the transformed column is

$$
2(1+y)e^{ax+xd}
$$

with the outgoing carry bit flipped for $t=0$, or the incoming carry bit flipped for $t=1$. This is a local coefficient identity. For the full product, the linear exterior factors satisfy

$$
\prod_i(1+y_i)
=\left(1+\sum_i y_i\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i<j}y_iy_j\right).
$$

The identity follows by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula because each commutator of two linear forms is quadratic and commutes with the others. The entire product consequently splits into two parity-definite Gaussian terms. Bit flips and the contractions on adjacent carry ports preserve the matchgate property. The transformed open arithmetic signature is a sum of at most two matchgates, for any length.

For the earlier repeated active family, two terms are also necessary under arbitrary pair bases. The matrix rank of an isolated block across its middle column versus the other two columns is three, and pair bases preserve this rank. For a parity-definite Gaussian, move the middle column to one side by a fermionic permutation. Its sign is $(-1)^{p_Ap_B}$ for exterior parts $A,B,C$. Total parity expresses $p_B$ in terms of $p_A,p_C$, so the sign is a diagonal change on the other side of the cut. The ordinary cut rank is therefore a power of two, by [Botero and Reznik](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0404176). The separator argument extends to pair bases because the two parity cap planes stay complementary.

## The transformed equality signature

The above two-term representation applies to $AU$. For the same explicit $A$, closing independent triangles of selection equalities gives a dual signature with much larger rank. Take three columns with target bits $(1,1,0)$ and cycle edges on the three positions. Let $D_t=4A_t^{-\mathsf T}$. Up to a nonzero factor, the transformed triangle has coefficients

$$
\eta(b_0,b_1,b_2)=
\sum_{e_0,e_1,e_2\in\{0,1\}}
\prod_{j=0}^2D_{t_j}[b_j,e_j+2e_{j-1}],
$$

with the indices of $e$ taken modulo three. Its cumulative Clifford ranks through degrees zero to three are at least $1,13,63,64$, as witnessed by elimination modulo $1009$ on integer coefficient columns. For $r$ contiguous triangles these give

$$
R_E\ge\left(\frac{1+12z+50z^2+z^3}{(1+z)^6}\right)^r>1.89^r
\quad\text{at }z=3/10.
$$

The family has an immediate subset solution selecting the weight three in each triangle at its scale. The bound isolates the cost of representing the dual equality signature as a separate sum. It leaves open contractions that exploit the two signatures together.
